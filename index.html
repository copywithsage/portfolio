Sleep & wellness product · side-sleeper pain point
Challenge: turn a mundane, everyday complaint into a native, scroll-stopping hook — the kind of video that reads like a real person talking, not a scripted ad.
Script — Angle 1 of 3

"I used three pillows every night for five years and my neck still hurts every single morning."

So I'm a side sleeper and every night I have this whole routine where I stack two pillows, fold the top one over so it's higher, shove one between my knees, and tuck the corner under for extra lift. My boyfriend calls it my nest and honestly I can't argue with that because it takes me a solid two minutes just to get into position.

And even with all of that I'd still wake up with a stiff neck and a sore shoulder because half the time the pillows had shifted apart during the night. What I didn't realize is that when you sleep on your side there's this gap between your shoulder and your head, and regular pillows just can't hold that height through the night no matter how many you stack together.

Then I found this cube shaped memory foam pillow that's one solid block designed to fill that exact gap. By the end of the week I went from three pillows and a two minute ritual to one pillow and just lying down like a normal person. If you have a whole pillow routine every night and you're still waking up sore, look up the Pillow Cube.

Creative Brief
Verso Skincare — Retinol 8 Positioning
Swedish science-led skincare brand · premium retinol line
Self-initiated brief — built on independent research to show how I'd approach Verso's positioning, not a live campaign.
Challenge: most readers have already tried retinol and quit, burned by irritation and overwhelmed by ten-step routines. The brief had to make "less" read as expert, not cheap, before a single ingredient claim would land.
Angle considered, and cut

The obvious move was to sound like every other retinol launch — lead with the transformation, the science, the reassurance. But scan enough retinol threads and a pattern shows up fast: every brand talks about the result it promises. Almost none talk about the part in between — the irritation, the purging, the weeks of trial and error before skin adjusts. That silence was the real opening, not another performance claim.

Strategic angle

Insight — the audience isn't short on retinol options, they're short on trust: everyone reading has tried it before and been burned, so another ingredient claim just repeats a promise they've already stopped believing. That's why the brief couldn't open with proof, it had to open with permission — a confession instead of a claim: "You're probably doing too much."

From there, the science backs up the confession instead of leading with it: retinol as the "gold standard," reframed as finally gentle enough to use. The reader isn't sold a product first, they're handed a reason to trust the person selling it.

Research note
Across Reddit and Quora skincare threads, the same story kept repeating: people weren't quitting retinol because it stopped working, they were quitting because nobody warned them what the first few weeks actually feel like. I recognized the pattern because I've dealt with it myself.
